November in your Central Florida garden

Hello from Shaley Gardens. November in Ocala, Marion County, and The Villages is a planting month. We are in USDA zones 9a and 9b. At the Ocala station, NOAA’s median first 32°F freeze is December 12 and the median last is February 25. About one year in ten, the first comes by November 21 or the last comes after March 21.

What to plant now

UF/IFAS Gardening Solutions calls Central Florida the land between State Road 40 and State Road 70. State Road 40 runs through Ocala. North of that road, also check the North Florida list. The Villages sits south of Ocala and fits the Central Florida list.

In November, these Central Florida vegetables are easy to transplant: arugula, beets, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, cauliflower, Chinese cabbage, collards, endive, kale, kohlrabi, lettuce, sugarcane, and Swiss chard. Transplant celery, mustard, pineapple, potatoes, and spinach with care. Sow carrots, bulbing and bunching onions, English peas, radish, and turnips. December uses that same list. This is not the month for a big new tomato planting. Tomatoes and peppers are hurt by frost.

The University of Florida fall herb list includes parsley, dill, thyme, oregano, sage, and chives. Full-sun flowers named for November in Central Florida include alyssum, calendula, dianthus, petunia, snapdragon, and viola.

Lawn care

November is a quiet month for fertilizer. The St. Augustinegrass, bahiagrass, and zoysiagrass guides put the first feeding of the year in early April in central Florida and mid-April in north Florida. In those turf guides, north Florida is anything north of Ocala. A late feeding makes tender growth that a freeze can burn.

Mow less often as growth slows, and leave the clippings. If bahiagrass turns brown in a drought and you have no sprinkler, leave it alone until water returns.

Zoysia seed is best sown from April through July. November is outside that window. North of Ocala, fall seeding is a poor idea because young plants may not be ready for cold. In deep shade, a Florida-Friendly groundcover may fit better than more sod. Read Best lawn grasses for sandy Ocala soil.

Freeze prep

Freezing weather normally occurs in north and central Florida. The night before, water the soil if it is dry so it can hold a little heat. Do not leave the bed soggy. Cover plants with frost cloth, a sheet, or a quilt that reaches the ground, and keep the cloth off the leaves. Do not lay plastic on the foliage. Take covers off once the temperature is above freezing, and vent them on a sunny day. LED holiday lights do not add meaningful heat. Bring pots in, or group them and mulch them. Turn the irrigation off.

Afterward, water if the wind dried the plants, and wait to prune cold-damaged growth until another freeze is unlikely. Green tissue under the bark is alive. Brown tissue is not. The longer steps are in Protecting your plants from a Central Florida freeze.

One quick tip

Let the lawn tell you when to water. On an established St. Augustinegrass or bahiagrass lawn, water when the blades fold, turn blue-gray, or keep your footprints. Then give about one-half to three-quarters of an inch, and wait until it asks again. Follow your local watering rules.
