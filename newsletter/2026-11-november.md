# November in your Central Florida garden

Draft only. Do not send this note, and do not publish it on the website.

Hello from Shaley Gardens. November in Ocala, Marion County, and The Villages is a planting month. The days are kinder than August, and the cool-season garden is the one to tend. We are in USDA zones 9a and 9b, and a freeze can still show up.

## What to plant now

UF/IFAS Gardening Solutions calls Central Florida the land between State Road 40 and State Road 70. State Road 40 runs through Ocala. North of that road, also check the North Florida list. The Villages sits south of Ocala and fits the Central Florida list.

In November, these Central Florida vegetables are easy to transplant: arugula, beets, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, cauliflower, Chinese cabbage, collards, endive, kale, kohlrabi, lettuce, sugarcane, and Swiss chard. Transplant celery, mustard, pineapple, potatoes, and spinach with care. Sow carrots, bulbing and bunching onions, English peas, radish, and turnips. December uses that same list. This is not the month for a big new tomato planting. Tomatoes and peppers are hurt by frost.

The University of Florida fall herb list includes parsley, dill, coriander, thyme, oregano, sage, and chives. Cilantro is the leaf of the coriander plant. Full-sun flowers named for November in Central Florida include alyssum, calendula, dianthus, petunia, snapdragon, and viola.

Read more in [Fall and winter vegetables for Marion County](https://shaleygardens.com/fall-vegetables.html) and on the [planting calendar](https://shaleygardens.com/calendar.html).

## Lawn care

November is a quiet month for fertilizer. The St. Augustinegrass, bahiagrass, and zoysiagrass guides put the first feeding of the year in early April in central Florida and mid-April in north Florida. In those turf guides, north Florida is anything north of Ocala. A late feeding makes tender growth that a freeze can burn.

Mow less often as growth slows, and leave the clippings. At the right height they feed the lawn and do not build thatch. If bahiagrass turns brown in a drought and you have no sprinkler, leave it alone until water returns.

Zoysia seed is best sown from April through July. November is outside that window. North of Ocala, fall seeding is a poor idea because young plants may not be ready for cold.

Where the lawn struggles in deep shade or on very poor sand, a Florida-Friendly groundcover may fit better than more sod. Sunshine mimosa, perennial peanut, and frogfruit are described for our zones. Read [Best lawn grasses for sandy Ocala soil](https://shaleygardens.com/lawns.html).

## Freeze prep

Freezing weather normally occurs in north and central Florida. The night before, water the soil if it is dry so it can hold a little heat. Do not leave the bed soggy. Cover plants with frost cloth, a sheet, or a quilt that reaches the ground, and keep the cloth off the leaves. Do not lay plastic on the foliage. Bring pots in, or group them and mulch them. Turn the irrigation off. A home sprinkler is not a farm freeze system.

Afterward, water if the wind dried the plants, and wait to prune until you can see live wood. Green tissue under the bark is alive. Brown tissue is not. The longer steps are in [Protecting your plants from a Central Florida freeze](https://shaleygardens.com/freeze.html).

## One quick tip

Let the lawn tell you when to water. On an established St. Augustinegrass or bahiagrass lawn, water when the blades fold, turn blue-gray, or keep your footprints. Then give about one-half to three-quarters of an inch, and wait until it asks again. Follow your local watering rules.
