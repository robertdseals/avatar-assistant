December is cool-season prime time in Central Florida: short days, fewer bugs and happy greens. It's also our first real freeze month, so keep frost cloth close. ★ = keto-friendly pick

🌱 Start in Trays (transplant in 4–6 weeks)

  • Broccoli ★, cabbage ★, cauliflower ★ for January planting
  • Lettuce ★, Swiss chard ★, Chinese cabbage ★
  • Bunching onions, parsley
  • Late December: start tomato and pepper seeds indoors for transplanting after the last freeze (median last freeze Feb 25; as late as Mar 21 about one year in ten)

🪴 Transplant Now

  • Broccoli ★, Brussels sprouts ★, cabbage ★, cauliflower ★
  • Collards ★, kale ★, kohlrabi ★, Chinese cabbage ★
  • Lettuce ★, Swiss chard ★, celery ★ (slow, 3+ months; keep moist)
  • Herbs: parsley, cilantro, thyme, sage, rosemary
  • Winter color: pansies, petunias, snapdragons

🌾 Direct Sow

  • Radish ★, turnips ★ (greens too), mustard ★
  • Spinach ★, arugula ★, endive/escarole ★
  • Carrots, beets ★ (greens), English peas
  • Bulbing and bunching onions
  • Irish potatoes: certified seed potatoes only, not store-bought

🧰 Garden Maintenance

  • Water less: check soil first and follow your local watering days. On a low-pressure well, hand-water the day before a freeze, not the night of.
  • Keep 2–3 in. of mulch; keep it a few inches off stems
  • Rake oak leaves into beds and compost
  • Skip lawn fertilizer until spring (Marion County: not before mid-April); mow less and leave clippings
  • Scout brassicas for caterpillars (hand-pick or Bt)
  • Clean & sharpen tools; note what grew well this fall

❄️ Seasonal Note: Freeze Watch & El Niño

NOAA's 1991–2020 normals put Ocala's median first freeze (32°F) around Dec 12, and about one year in ten it comes by Nov 21. When lows near 32°F, drape frost cloth or old sheets all the way to the ground (never plastic on leaves), cover lettuce, mustard and chard, and protect bananas, young citrus, avocado and papaya. Move pots against the house, water dry soil the day before, and uncover once it warms above freezing. Don't prune cold-damaged growth until late February or early March, when new growth shows. Hold tomatoes, peppers, eggplant and squash until late winter. This strong El Niño winter may bring more rain and storms: watch drainage and fungus, and never fertilize before heavy rain.
